The Process

How we work with you

From the first phone call through to the day you get the keys, this is what engaging Rentoule Projects looks like. Have a read before we talk. It helps you turn up to that first conversation with the right questions ready.

By Jeff Rentoule · 8 min read · No signup required

This page is not a sales pitch. It is a plain-language explanation of what happens when you engage Rentoule Projects, so you know exactly what to expect before we speak.

I'm Jeff Rentoule. I own and run Rentoule Projects, a small residential building business in Canberra. Over 19 years in the industry and 5 years running my own business, I have refined the way I take a client through a project. This is that process, in the order it actually happens, with the mechanics I would explain to you sitting across the table.

Feel free to read this end to end, dive into the sections you are interested in, or refer back to it as we work together.

Before you reach out, some questions to sit with

Every good project starts with the client being clear on what they actually want. I really want to work with you to make that clear from the first conversation. You will have questions, and I encourage you to bring them to our first meeting so we can properly map out what your goal is. Many clients find there are areas where they are still unclear, and it is important to communicate those areas with me. It is a signal about where we need to spend our time.

  • What is the scope of work you have in mind?

    Bathroom only, kitchen only, full house renovation, extension, knockdown rebuild. If you are not sure yet, that is fine. But have a sense of how you want the finished home to feel differently to the way it does now.

  • What are you actually trying to achieve?

    Not the renovation itself. The reason for the renovation. For example, more space for a growing family, a house that works for someone ageing in place, a kitchen you can actually cook in, or a home that feels like home rather than a starter house you never left. This is the part that shapes everything downstream.

  • Do you have more than one option you are considering?

    Renovate or rebuild. Extend up or extend out. Stay in this house or sell and buy. Renovate the whole house or do the kitchen properly first. If you are weighing options, tell me what pulled you toward one over the other. I am always happy to share my professional recommendation.

  • What is your realistic budget, and what is your dream budget?

    Everyone has a budget. Both the realistic and the dream number matter. The realistic one keeps the project honest. The dream one tells me what you would spend on if the money was there, which helps me flag where the biggest levers are. The question is how firm each of those numbers is.

  • Do you have drawings, or is this still an idea?

    Idea, sketch on the back of a napkin, concept from a designer, full construction drawings, half-finished DA. There is no wrong answer. It tells me where in the process we are starting and what we need to line up next.

  • Have you spoken to any other builders?

    If yes, what were they like and what did you like or dislike about how they approached it. If no, what has held you back from getting more quotes. Both answers are useful. It is important to speak to multiple builders so you understand how your investment with them will be valued.

  • Who else is part of the decision?

    Partner, kids, parents, siblings, financial adviser. I want every decision-maker at the table from the start, so every voice can be heard and we can make swift decisions to keep your project on track. Best to loop them in early.

Take your time with these. If some of the answers move once you have thought about them, that is exactly the point. Reach out when you are ready and we will pick up from wherever you land.

The eight stages of working with us

Every project moves through the same eight stages. The scope changes, the timeline changes, the number of consultants changes, but the sequence does not. Understanding it up front means fewer surprises later. The bit that catches most people out is how much of the real work happens before construction ever starts. That is deliberate. All of the pre-construction work you see here exists so that when we do get to construction, the pricing is transparent, clear and locked in. That transparency is the thing I do differently.

Discovery call

~15 minutes · Free

We start with a short phone call. About fifteen minutes. Long enough for me to get a real read on what you are trying to build, short enough that it does not eat into your day. This is where the questions I asked you to sit with earlier come into play. I will ask about your scope, what you are trying to achieve, your rough budget and timeline, and where you are in your thinking. I do not quote at this stage. It is too early and any number I gave you would be inaccurate.

You get: a straight answer on whether we are the right builder for your project and whether the next step (a site visit) makes sense. I get: enough context to prepare properly if we do proceed. If it is not the right fit, I will tell you and often suggest a builder who is better matched.

Site visit

~1 hour on site · Free

Once we have agreed we are a good fit, I visit the site. I walk through what you are wanting to build with any drawings, sketches or concepts you have, take measurements where they help, and take the time to understand the site properly. Orientation, access, existing structure, neighbouring conditions. The things that shape what is possible and what is not.

Just as important, I want to hear and see in person what you like and do not like about your current home. What is not working. What frustrates you every day. What you love and want to keep. What you hope the changes will actually improve. How the house feels now and how you want it to feel after the work. This is what "making your house your home" actually means in practice. It is the part most builders skip. It is also the part that shapes the best design decisions later.

Bring any drawings you have, even rough ones, and be ready to talk through the constraints and non-negotiables of the project. Be open to walking me through what daily life looks like in the house now. This is where I get everything I need to prepare a ballpark that is honest, not aspirational.

Ballpark proposal

Within 2 weeks of site visit · Free

After the site visit, I take up to two weeks to prepare your ballpark proposal. It is an approximate category-level price range designed to give you a clear indication of what your scope will cost. Not a single fixed number. It includes what is in scope, what is not, an indicative timeline, and what the Preliminary Services Agreement covers if you decide to move forward.

I send the ballpark through the evening before we meet, so you have time to read it, sit with it, and jot down any questions overnight. We then meet in person to walk through it together. I go through what is included, what is not, where the numbers came from, and the levers you can pull to adjust the costings so you get the best value for your money. We also talk through your options. Different scope splits, or delivering the project in stages instead of all at once, and what each of those would do to your cost and your timeline.

We finish that meeting talking about next steps. If the range fits and you want to keep going, I explain what the PSA covers and roughly what happens next. If the range does not fit, we talk about how to scale scope back. Either way, you leave clear on where you stand.

Preliminary Services Agreement (PSA)

On average 6 to 8 weeks · Fee credited against contract

This is where the real pre-construction work happens. The PSA is a short written agreement that formalises the pre-contract work I do to turn your ballpark into a fixed-price contract, and it locks in your build slot in my calendar so we can hit the ground running once we get to contract.

The PSA has a fee, tiered based on the pathway your project needs. I will always tell you what your specific fee is before you sign. It is credited in full against your building contract on signing, so if you proceed to construction the PSA effectively costs nothing on top of the build price. It only stands alone if you decide not to proceed.

During the PSA period, four things run in parallel:

  • Design lock-in. If your project needs an architect, this is where we bring one in. I collaborate with them throughout the planning stage, attending design meetings, providing buildability input and keeping the cost plan honest as the drawings develop.
  • Consultant coordination. Engineer, energy assessor, surveyor, draftsperson, certifier, whoever else your project needs. Their fees are paid direct by you.
  • Selections begin. See the next stage for what selections actually means and the three pathways for how they run.
  • Approval lodgement. If your project needs Building Approval or Development Approval, we can lodge during the PSA once the required documents are back and you are comfortable with the budget. This runs the approval process in parallel with selections, saving weeks off your pre-construction time.

Selections and verification

During PSA period · In-person at trade suppliers

This is the part most builders leave to the client to do alone. I do not. Selections drive the price more than almost anything else, so we do them together, in person, at the trade suppliers. Every cost-carrying selection is chosen with someone in the room calling out the cost impact live as we go. That way we know what the number is going to look like before we lock anything in.

Quick plain-English translation

"Selections" in the industry is the term for every material, fitting, fixture and finish choice you make about your home. In practice this means paint colours, tapware (taps, mixers, shower heads), lighting fixtures, joinery design (kitchen cabinets, wardrobes, vanities) and their finishes (colours, handles, level of detail), plus flooring, tiling, benchtops, splashbacks, appliances, door and window hardware. "Finishes" is the shorthand for the visible surface and material choices within all of that. If either word ever comes up and it is not clear what I mean, ask. Most first-time renovators have not needed to know these terms before, and there is no reason to pretend otherwise.

You have three pathways for who runs selections with you:

  • Your architect. Most architects include interior design as part of their service, so if we have an architect on the project this is often the most natural option.
  • A standalone interior designer. Engage one directly and they attend the trade supplier appointments with us to drive the interior decisions.
  • Me directly. If you have a clear picture of what you want, I can run selections with you. To be straight though, my selections help is limited. I can guide you through the process, keep the cost impact honest, and stop you from making expensive mistakes. But if you do not have a clear vision, you will get a much better outcome working with a specialist. That is what they do every day.

Final quote and fixed-price contract

10% deposit at contract signing

Once selections are locked and trade pricing is verified, I come back to you with the final quote. Every line is firm cost, tied to real product and real trade rates. You also get a clear construction program showing when each phase happens, and a payment schedule that ties progress claims to construction milestones. Nothing is left to figure out later.

This is the checkpoint moment. If we are inside budget, we push through to contract. If we are over, we scale back together, either by adjusting scope or going back to suppliers to look at alternatives. If we are under, this is your chance to add scope back in.

Once the numbers work, the final quote is signed into a fixed-price building contract, either MBA or HIA depending on the project. A 10 percent deposit is paid at contract signing. This is the number you have from here to handover, adjusted only by written variations you approve.

Building Approval

If required · Certifier engaged and paid direct

Most extensions and new builds need Building Approval or Development Approval. As covered in stage 4, we usually lodge for it during the PSA once the drawings and consultants have delivered what the certifier needs and you are comfortable with the budget. That way it runs concurrently with selections instead of stretching out afterwards.

Lodgement requires engaging a certifier. Their fee is paid direct by you and varies with project scale, which is why I cannot lock it at ballpark. Once approval is issued and the contract is signed, we mobilise the site.

Pure renovation projects that do not need Building Approval skip this stage and go straight from contract to construction.

Construction and handover

Site manager daily · 90-day defect period

Our site manager is on your site daily. I am across every stage but not on site every day. Our site manager runs the delivery, I run the project. You get regular progress updates, a written variation for anything that changes cost or scope, and site visits at your major milestones.

We hand over the finished home with certificates, warranties, and a walkthrough of everything that matters. Then we enter a 90-day defect period where I am your first call for anything that needs looking at.

After the defect period ends, if something comes up down the track, I am always happy to help. Either directly if it is something I can sort, or by pointing you in the direction of the right person if it is not. Canberra is a small town. I want you to feel like you can trust the partnership we build together, and know that you are still valued long after the keys are handed over.

A quick note

This is the process end to end. The eight stages do not change from project to project. What changes inside each stage is a function of your scope: how many consultants get involved, how much design work sits inside the PSA, whether Building Approval is needed, how long selections take, how long construction runs. The order stays the same. The content shifts to match what your project actually needs.

How the money works

Transparency around money is something I pride myself on. No cost-plus. No hidden margin. No surprise increases. Fixed price means fixed price.

The discovery call is free. The site visit is free. The ballpark proposal is free.

The Preliminary Services Agreement has a fee, and that fee is tiered based on your project's pathway. Smaller projects, renovation-only work, and projects requiring Building Approval each sit at different price points. I will always tell you exactly what your specific PSA fee is before you sign, and I quote it in writing on the ballpark.

Whatever the fee, it is credited in full against your building contract on signing. Your total spend across PSA and construction is unchanged. The fee only stands alone if you decide not to proceed after the PSA.

Consultant fees are paid direct by you. Engineer, energy assessor, surveyor, draftsperson, certifier, architect if we engage one. Their fees vary too much with project scale to fix at ballpark stage.

Once the fixed-price contract is signed, the price is locked. Only written variations that you approve change the number.

What makes this different

There is nothing dramatic about this process. It is what every builder should do. But most do not, so it is worth naming what makes this way of working actually different.

  • Fixed-price contracts only. No cost-plus. No "we will work it out as we go".
  • Selections happen in person with me at the trade suppliers. You never guess what a tile choice costs.
  • The number firms up progressively across three quotes. Ballpark, revised after selections, final. So you never see a $50,000 jump in a single conversation.
  • Every cost you pay someone else. Consultants, certifiers, council fees. Passed through at cost.
  • Our site manager is on site every day. I am across every project. The team is small on purpose.
  • Every variation is written before it happens. If it is not written, it is not chargeable.

Common questions clients ask

What happens if the final quote comes in over budget?

We scale back together. Either we adjust the scope. Drop the ensuite renovation, defer the deck, choose a different cladding. Or we go back to suppliers to look at alternatives on the biggest cost lines. The final quote checkpoint exists specifically so we can do this before you are locked into a contract.

How firm is a fixed-price contract really?

As firm as the contract. The price cannot change without a written variation that you sign. If something in the scope or site conditions changes, we write it up, price it, you approve or reject it before the work happens. If there is no variation, there is no price change. Full stop.

Why the PSA instead of just quoting off drawings?

Because a quote off drawings is a guess dressed up as a number. Real prices come from real trades pricing real scope with real selections. The PSA is how we get to a real number honestly. Cheaper for you in the long run than a bad guess.

How long does the whole thing take before construction starts?

From discovery call to site start, expect roughly two to three months for a straight renovation and four to six months for an extension or new build that needs Building Approval. The PSA phase is the biggest chunk because that is where consultants, drawings, selections and trade pricing all get pulled together. It is time well spent. It is the difference between a fixed price you can trust and a starting number that drifts.

Do you take on smaller jobs or only large ones?

Anything from a proper bathroom renovation up to a large custom build. The PSA fee tier accommodates smaller projects. What I will not take on is jobs where the scope is unclear or the client is not sure they want to proceed. Life is too short and my calendar is too tight.

What if I have already engaged a designer or architect?

That is normal, and it is often better. Bring them in early. I collaborate with your designer through the PSA process, attend design meetings if useful, and provide buildability and cost input as the drawings develop. Two heads on the design side almost always makes a better project.

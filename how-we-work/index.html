A note from Jeff19 years in the Canberra building industry, the last five running Rentoule Projects.

The projects that go badly rarely go wrong on site. They go wrong earlier, when a price was guessed, a decision was left for later, or nobody was quite sure who was doing what.

So we put our effort into that early part. This page is a plain explanation of how. Read it end to end, or jump to the sections you care about.

01 Principles

Four principles, and the worries they answer

People rarely come to us worried about tiles. They worry about the price moving, about decisions piling up, about being left in the dark, and about who picks up the phone once the job is done. Here is how we think about each.

“Will the price we sign up to be the price we pay?”

Principle one

Think Before We Build

A successful build starts well before construction. We get involved early to test ideas, understand costs, identify risks and help the design develop into something both beautiful and buildable. The work happens up front, so the scope is clear and the price is fixed before construction begins.

In practiceDesign, consultants, selections and trade pricing are worked through under a Preliminary Services Agreement, so your fixed price is built from decisions you have made and pricing we have checked.

“Will we be listened to, or just sold something?”

Principle two

Build It Together

The best homes come from genuine collaboration. Clients, architects, consultants, trades and builders all bring something valuable. We listen, share our experience and work as one team toward a home you will love living in.

In practiceWe work alongside your architect or designer, give buildability and cost input as the drawings develop, and make cost-carrying selections with you in person at trade suppliers.

“How will we keep track of everything that has to happen?”

Principle three

Calm Over Chaos

Building is complicated. Your experience of it should not feel that way. We keep the work organised, the next steps clear and the communication steady, so you know where things stand and what is needed from you.

In practiceDuring pre-construction your client portal shows progress, documents, outstanding actions and selections in one place. See the portal.

“What happens if something isn’t right, or after we move back in?”

Principle four

Stand Behind Our Work

We take responsibility for what we do and follow through on what we promise. When challenges come up, we deal with them openly and work toward the right solution. Our relationship with you does not end when construction does.

In practiceAny change to cost or scope is written up and approved by you before the work happens. After handover there is a 90-day defect period with us as your first call.

“Our site manager runs the delivery. I run the project. The team is small on purpose.”
Jeff Rentoule, Director

02 The process at a glance

From the first call to the keys

Every project follows the same sequence. What changes is how long each part takes and how many people are involved. Look at how much happens before construction starts. That is deliberate.

The first conversations cost nothing

The discovery call, site consultation and ballpark proposal are free. By the end of them you know whether we are the right builder and roughly what your project will cost.

The real work runs in parallel

During the PSA, design, consultants, selections and pricing move together rather than one after another. Where your project needs Building Approval, we can lodge during this period too.

The price is fixed before we build

By the time you sign a building contract, the scope is defined, the selections are made and the trade pricing has been checked. Changes after that go through written variations you approve.

03 Step by step

What happens, and when

The same process in more detail, in three parts. Every project is a little different, so timeframes here are typical rather than promised.

I

Getting to know each other

No cost to you

Enough time for both of us to decide whether this is the right project and the right fit, before anyone commits to anything.

  1. 01

    Initial enquiry

    • Website, phone or email

    Tell us a little about your home and what you have in mind. A few lines is plenty. We will come back to you to arrange a short call.

  2. 02

    Discovery call

    • About 15 minutes
    • Free

    A short phone call so I can get a real read on what you are trying to build: your scope, what you are hoping to achieve, a rough budget and timeline, and where you are in your thinking. I do not quote at this stage. It is too early, and any number I gave you would not be accurate.

    You get a straight answer on whether we are the right builder for your project and whether a site visit makes sense. If we are not the right fit, I will tell you, and often suggest a builder who is better matched.

  3. 03

    Site consultation

    • About an hour on site
    • Free

    I walk through your home with you and any drawings, sketches or concepts you have. Orientation, access, existing structure, neighbouring conditions: the things that shape what is possible.

    Just as important, I want to see what you like and do not like about the house now. What frustrates you every day, what you love and want to keep, and how you want the place to feel when we are finished. That conversation shapes the best design decisions later.

  4. 04

    Ballpark proposal

    • Usually within two weeks
    • Free
    • Walked through in person

    An approximate, category-level price range for your scope. Not a single fixed number. It sets out what is in scope and what is not, an indicative timeline, and what the Preliminary Services Agreement would cover, including its fee.

    I send it the evening before we meet so you can read it and note your questions. Then we go through it together: where the numbers come from, the levers that change them, and options such as a different scope split or staging the work.

    You get a clear picture of where you stand. If the range fits, we talk about the PSA. If it does not, we talk honestly about how to bring the scope back.

II

The Preliminary Services Agreement

Paid · credited in full if you proceed

Often around six to eight weeks, longer where there is significant design work or a Building Approval to prepare.

The PSA is where your ballpark becomes a fixed price. It is a short written agreement covering the pre-construction work needed to get there properly.

The PSA fee is tiered to suit your project and quoted in writing before you sign. If you go ahead to a building contract with us, the fee is credited in full against it. It only stands alone if you decide not to proceed.

What runs during the PSA

ADesign development

If your project needs an architect or designer, this is where the design is resolved. We attend design meetings where useful, give buildability input and keep the cost plan honest as the drawings develop.

BConsultant coordination

Engineer, energy assessor, surveyor, certifier and anyone else your project needs. We brief them, chase them and pull their work together. Their fees are generally paid by you directly.

CSelections

Every material, fitting and finish that carries a cost is chosen and confirmed, with the cost impact discussed as you go rather than discovered later.

DDetailed pricing

Real trades price real scope against your actual selections. This is what turns a range into a number we can stand behind.

EApproval lodgement

Where Building Approval or a DA is needed, we can lodge once the required documents are ready and you are comfortable with the budget, so approval runs alongside selections rather than after them.

+Your client portal

All of this is tracked in your pre-construction client portal, so you can see what is done, what is next and what is waiting on you.

What we mean by “selections”
Every material, fitting, fixture and finish in your home: paint colours, tapware, lighting, joinery and its finishes and handles, flooring, tiles, benchtops, splashbacks, appliances, door and window hardware. If a term ever comes up that is not clear, ask. Most people have never needed these words before.

Three ways to run selections

With your architect

Many architects include interior design in their service, which makes this the natural choice when one is on the project.

With an interior designer

Engaged by you directly. They come to the trade supplier appointments with us and lead the interior decisions.

With us directly

If you have a clear picture of what you want. We will guide the process and keep the cost impact honest. If you do not have a clear vision yet, a specialist will get you a better result.

III

Contract, approval and construction

Fixed-price contract

The thinking is done. From here, the job is to build what was agreed, well, and to keep you informed while we do it.

  1. 05

    Final quote and fixed-price contract

    • MBA or HIA contract
    • 10% deposit on signing

    Once selections are locked and trade pricing is checked, you receive the final quote, a construction program showing when each phase happens, and a payment schedule that ties progress claims to construction milestones.

    This is the checkpoint. If we are inside budget, we move to contract. If we are over, we scale back together, by adjusting scope or looking at alternatives on the biggest cost lines. If we are under, you can add scope back in. Signing the contract and paying the deposit is also what locks in your build slot.

  2. 06

    Building Approval

    • Where required
    • Certifier engaged and paid by you

    Most extensions and new homes need Building Approval, and some projects also need Development Approval. Because we usually lodge during the PSA, approval often runs alongside selections and pricing. Internal renovations that do not need approval move straight from contract to construction.

  3. 07

    Construction

    • Site manager on site daily
    • Written variations only

    Our site manager is on your site every day and runs the delivery. I am across every stage and run the project. You get regular progress updates, site visits at the major milestones, and a written variation for anything that would change cost or scope, approved by you before the work happens.

  4. 08

    Handover and aftercare

    • 90-day defect period
    • Certificates and warranties

    We hand over your home with its certificates and warranties and walk you through everything that matters. Then there is a 90-day defect period where we are your first call for anything that needs attention.

    After that, if something comes up, we are still happy to help, either directly or by pointing you to the right person. Canberra is a small town. We want you to feel looked after long after the keys change hands.

04 Pre-Construction Client Portal

Pre-construction, made visible

Before a fixed price is possible, a long list of things has to happen. Drawings developed, consultants briefed, reports returned, products chosen, approval documents gathered, and every cost-carrying decision priced. Most of that work is invisible to clients. Our portal makes it visible.

Not software we sell. Part of the service.

The portal comes with your Preliminary Services Agreement. It is how we run and report on the pre-construction work for your project, and where you take part in it.

See where your project is up to

Progress across design, consultants, selections, pricing and Building Approval, whenever you want to check, without waiting for an update email.

Know what is next, and who it is waiting on

Outstanding actions with an owner and a date, whether they sit with you, with us or with a consultant. When something needs you, it is obvious.

Documents and Building Approval in one place

Project documents, plus a checklist of what is needed for Building Approval and where each item is up to, so lodgement is not held up by one missing form.

Take part in your selections

Review the selections proposed for each room, confirm the ones you are happy with and flag the ones you want to change, with a clear record of what has been agreed.

From agreed decisions to a construction specification

Once selections are agreed, the portal helps turn them into the specification we price and build from, so the decisions you made carry through to construction.

One place to look

Progress, documents and decisions live together, instead of being scattered across email threads, attachments and phone calls.

Fewer surprises at the final quote

Because decisions are made and recorded as you go, the final quote reflects choices you have already seen and agreed.

A clear record of what was agreed

Agreed selections feed into the specification, which gives you, us and our trades the same reference point once construction starts.

06 Is it a fit?

Who we are right for

We are not trying to be the cheapest builder in Canberra. We are trying to be the one you are glad you chose two years after the job is finished. That suits some people better than others.

We tend to be a good fit if you

  • are planning a major renovation, extension or custom home and want it done properly
  • would rather have a fixed price you can trust than the lowest number on day one
  • want to be involved in decisions, with a builder alongside you
  • see the value in planning before construction starts

We are probably not the right builder if you

  • are mainly looking for the cheapest quote
  • want a fixed price off concept drawings within a few days
  • would prefer to skip pre-construction and work it out as we go

For context: most of our clients invest somewhere between $300,000 and $900,000, and some projects are considerably larger. Your ballpark proposal will tell you where yours is likely to sit.

Questions worth thinking about before we talk

You do not need answers to all of these. Where you are unsure is useful too. It tells us where to spend our time together. Bring them to the discovery call.

  1. What scope of work do you have in mind?

    Bathroom, kitchen, whole house, extension, knockdown rebuild. If you are not sure, have a sense of how you want the finished home to feel.

  2. What are you really trying to achieve?

    Not the renovation, the reason for it. More room for a growing family, a home to age in, a kitchen you can cook in properly.

  3. Are you weighing more than one option?

    Renovate or rebuild, extend up or out, stay or sell. Tell us what is pulling you each way. We are always happy to give a professional view.

  4. What is your realistic budget, and your dream budget?

    Both matter. One keeps the project honest. The other shows where the biggest levers are.

  5. Do you have drawings, or is it still an idea?

    A sketch, a concept, full drawings or a half-finished DA. There is no wrong answer. It tells us where we are starting.

  6. Have you spoken to other builders?

    What did you like or dislike about how they approached it? Speaking to more than one builder is a good idea.

  7. Who else is part of the decision?

    Partner, family, a financial adviser. It helps to have every decision-maker involved from the start.

07 Questions

Questions clients ask us

If yours is not here, ask it on the discovery call. For a deeper look at costs, approvals and planning in the ACT, read our free Canberra Renovation Guide.

Why pay for a PSA instead of getting a quote off drawings?

Because a quote off drawings alone has to guess at everything the drawings do not say: the selections, the consultant requirements, the site details. Real prices come from real trades pricing a defined scope with real selections. The PSA is how we get there honestly, and if you go ahead the fee is credited in full against your contract.

What happens if the final quote comes in over budget?

We scale back together. Either we adjust the scope, such as deferring a deck or choosing a different cladding, or we go back to suppliers for alternatives on the biggest cost lines. The final quote is a checkpoint that exists so this can happen before you are committed to a contract.

How firm is the fixed price?

The contract price changes only through a written variation that you approve. If something in the scope or site conditions changes, we write it up and price it, and you approve or decline it before the work happens.

How long before construction starts?

As a rough guide, from discovery call to site start, expect around two to three months for a renovation that does not need approval, and four to six months for an extension or new home that needs Building Approval. The PSA is the largest part of that, and it is time well spent.

We already have an architect or designer. Is that a problem?

Not at all. It is common, and often better. Bring them in early. We work with your designer through the PSA, attend design meetings where useful, and give buildability and cost input as the drawings develop.

Do we need to use the client portal?

The portal is part of how we run pre-construction under the PSA, so it is there for you from the start. It does not replace talking to us. It means you can see where things are up to between conversations, and find what you need without searching through emails.

Fireplaces and Wood Heaters in Canberra: The 2026 Rules Explained

Written by Jeff Rentoule2 July 2026, updated 30 October 20268 min read

In most Canberra suburbs you can still install a new wood heater, provided it is a certified model and you get building approval. Three areas are restricted, the rules on the appliance itself have tightened, and the long-term direction is away from wood and gas. Existing fireplaces can stay. Gas fireplaces are still possible on an existing gas connection but not on a new one. Electric and bioethanol are where most new fireplace projects end up.

I'm Jeff Rentoule. I've been in the Canberra building industry for 19 years, the last 5 of those running Rentoule Projects, and few topics come up more often in client conversations than the fireplace question. Canberrans love a fireplace, and rightly so. Winter here is cold, real cold, and there's nothing like a wood fire when the frost is on the lawn. But the rules have moved, and a lot of what gets repeated about them is out of date or wrong. Here is where things stand, checked against the legislation and the Government's own statements, with sources at the end.

What the ACT Government announced (and what it has done)

To understand where we are now, you need a quick history.

In January 2023, the ACT Commissioner for Sustainability and the Environment released a report recommending the government phase out wood heaters from all ACT suburbs by 2045, ban new installations everywhere, and require removal before sale. The report concluded there is "no safe level of air pollution for human health".

In August 2023, the ACT Government agreed in principle to most of the recommendations. It said extending the existing restriction on new wood heaters to the Tuggeranong Valley "appears timely and appropriate", and that it would consider doing so once air quality assessments, regulatory impact assessments and community consultation were complete. That is the line that got reported as a Tuggeranong ban. It was an intention, not a rule.

In September 2025, the Legislative Assembly voted against a motion that would have started community consultation on a phase-out plan. The Minister for Climate Change and the Environment described the 2023 commitments, including the Tuggeranong one, as "in principle" only.

In May 2026, the Minister told the Assembly that the current restrictions apply in Dunlop, East O'Malley and the Molonglo Valley, and that a regulatory impact assessment on any wider restriction will be progressed in 2026-27. Until that is done, there is no decision.

The practical effect: three restricted areas, no territory-wide ban, no removal-on-sale rule, and no legislated phase-out date. The 2045 target is a policy statement.

Where new wood heaters are restricted (as at 30 October 2026)

In three areas, the Crown lease for each block contains a clause that requires written approval from the Territory before a solid fuel heater can be installed. That approval is a development application, assessed on air quality grounds, and in practice it is rarely given. Treat these as no-go areas for a new wood heater:

  • Molonglo Valley (all suburbs except Wright)
  • Dunlop
  • East O'Malley

Everywhere else in Canberra, including Tuggeranong, Belconnen, Gungahlin, Woden, Weston Creek and the inner north and south, installing a solid fuel heater is exempt from development approval provided the flue meets the height rules in the Planning and Development Regulation 2008. New estates can carry the same lease clause as the three areas above, so if you are building on a new block, check the Crown lease before you plan a wood heater.

The restriction sits in the lease, not in a suburb list in the Environment Protection Act. That is why a certifier cannot sign off a wood heater in those three areas without the Territory's written approval, and why no other suburb is affected until its leases are.

Anywhere in the ACT, you always need:

  • A certified compliant appliance with a compliance plate. The ACT regulation requires a minimum overall efficiency of 60% and particulate emissions of no more than 1.5 g/kg (0.8 g/kg for a heater with a catalytic combustor), tested to AS/NZS 4012 and AS/NZS 4013. The Australian Standard itself was tightened to 1.0 g/kg in June 2025, so newer models are being certified to the tighter figure. Check the plate.
  • Installation in line with AS/NZS 2918 (clearances, flue, hearth)
  • Building approval from a private certifier covering the installation
  • Development approval only if the flue does not meet the exempt height rules, or if your lease requires it

One change that is law: since early 2025 it has been an offence in the ACT to install a non-compliant wood heater, not only to sell one. That catches second-hand heaters, heaters moved from another house, and the one your mate has in his shed. If it has no compliance plate, it cannot go in.

What about existing wood heaters?

If your home already has a working wood heater that was lawfully installed, you can keep using it. There is no requirement to remove it on sale. The 2023 recommendation that this happen was not adopted.

You can replace an existing wood heater with a new certified model anywhere outside the three restricted areas, with building approval. Inside those areas, a replacement is treated the same as a new installation and needs the Territory's written approval, so assume no.

The 2045 phase-out target, if it ever becomes regulation, would likely involve removal or replacement of existing heaters over time. Right now it is a policy statement, not law. The ACT Government does run a rebate for replacing a wood heater with efficient electric heating if you want to move early.

Gas fireplaces and the gas phase-out

The ACT is phasing out fossil fuel gas, with the goal of being all-electric by 2045. The rule that exists today is narrower than most people think. Since 8 December 2023, Evoenergy cannot provide a new gas network connection to a home in the ACT, with limited exemptions. That is the whole rule. There is no ban on gas appliances.

In practice for fireplaces, this means:

  • Existing home with a gas meter: you can keep using gas, service and replace your gas fireplace, and have a licensed gasfitter install a new one. You cannot increase the capacity of the connection or add a second supply point.
  • New build on a block with no gas: no gas connection, so no gas fireplace. Design all-electric from the start.
  • Knockdown rebuild or major renovation: this is the trap. If the gas connection has to be abolished to make the site safe during demolition or construction, reinstating it afterwards counts as a new connection and is not allowed. The house goes all-electric whether that was the plan or not. If keeping gas matters to you, raise it before demolition is scoped.
  • Extension where the meter is relocated: usually not a new connection, but confirm with Evoenergy before work starts.
  • Bottled LPG is not covered by the regulation.

What is coming: in August 2026 the Government opened consultation on whether to regulate gas appliances themselves, with options including limits on new gas space heating installations and switching off sections of the network from the mid-2030s. Nothing from that paper has been decided or legislated as at the date above. If you are planning a gas fireplace in an existing home, it is legal today. Whether it is a good 20-year decision is a different question, and my answer is usually no.

The alternatives that work well in Canberra

Three options have replaced wood and gas fireplaces in most of our recent projects.

Electric flame-effect fireplaces

The technology has improved a long way. The current generation of high-end electric fireplaces from brands like Dimplex Opti-V, Modern Flames and EcoSmart create flame illusions that, in person, are convincing. They throw no real heat by themselves but most include built-in heating elements that warm a room reasonably well.

  • Approval: treated as an appliance, not a combustion device. No wood heater or gas rules apply
  • Running cost: high if used as primary heating, moderate as ambience
  • Aesthetic: very good at the premium end
  • Cost installed: $4,000 to $15,000 depending on model and built-in cabinetry

Bioethanol (alcohol-fuelled) fireplaces

These burn liquid ethanol, produce a real flame, and generate genuine heat without a flue. No smoke, no chimney. They are regulated under the ACCC's mandatory standard for decorative alcohol-fuelled devices and must be certified.

  • Approval: not a solid fuel or gas appliance, so none of the rules above apply
  • Running cost: ethanol fuel is roughly $5 to $10 per hour of burn time
  • Aesthetic: real flame, very atmospheric
  • Cost installed: $3,000 to $20,000 for premium built-in units
  • Safety: the mandatory standard exists because of incidents. Buy certified, install professionally, never refuel while lit

Heat pump with a built-in feature wall

Not a fireplace, but worth flagging because it is where most of our clients end up when they prioritise heating performance over flame. A high-end reverse cycle unit hidden behind a designed feature wall, with a separate built-in flame element if desired. Best heating efficiency by a large margin, lowest running cost, no compliance issues.

What this means if you're planning a renovation

If a fireplace is on your wishlist, here's how to think about it.

Step 1. Check your Crown lease. If you are in Dunlop, East O'Malley or the Molonglo Valley (except Wright), or on a new estate block with the same clause, wood is off the table for a new installation. Anywhere else, it is available with building approval.

Step 2. If you have an existing wood heater you love and want to keep, that's fine. Note that it can affect the EER rating on a renovation if it is not insulated and sealed properly, so factor in airtightness work. Our EER ratings guide covers the interaction in more detail.

Step 3. If gas is on your list, find out early whether the connection will survive the works. Once it is abolished, it is gone.

Step 4. If you want a fireplace feature in a new build or extension, default to electric or bioethanol. Both are easier to approve, more flexible to place, and in line with where the ACT is heading.

Step 5. Have the conversation with your builder at design stage. The flue, hearth, structural support and ventilation all need to be designed in from the outset. Our builder selection guide and DA approval guide both cover this in more detail.

Frequently asked questions

Can I rip out my wood heater during a renovation and put a better one in?

Outside Dunlop, East O'Malley and the Molonglo Valley (except Wright), yes, with building approval and a certified compliant appliance. Inside those areas a replacement needs the Territory's written approval under your lease, which is rarely given. You can remove an existing heater anywhere without approval.

Are wood heaters banned in Tuggeranong?

No. There is no restriction on installing a certified wood heater in any Tuggeranong suburb beyond the building approval that applies everywhere in the ACT. The Government said in 2023 it was minded to extend the restriction to the Tuggeranong Valley, but it has not done so, and in May 2026 confirmed that any decision will follow a regulatory impact assessment in 2026-27.

Will I have to remove my wood heater before I sell my house in Canberra?

Not currently. The 2023 recommendation that removal be required at point of sale was not adopted. The 2045 phase-out target may eventually bring removal or replacement requirements, but there is no firm date or binding rule.

I'm building a new home in Dunlop, East O'Malley or the Molonglo Valley. Can I have a wood fireplace?

Not without written approval from the Territory under your Crown lease, and in practice that is rarely given. Wright is the exception in Molonglo. Look at a certified electric flame-effect unit, a bioethanol fireplace, or a reverse cycle heat pump behind a feature wall if a fireplace is important to your design.

Can I install a new gas fireplace in my Canberra renovation?

If your home already has a gas connection and it stays connected through the works, yes, through a licensed gasfitter. If the connection is abolished for demolition or construction, it cannot be reinstated and gas is off the table. New builds on unconnected blocks cannot get a connection. Plan for electric or bioethanol unless you have a connection you can keep.

Are outdoor fire pits and pizza ovens still allowed in Canberra?

Yes. The wood heater rules apply to indoor heating appliances. Outdoor wood fire pits, pizza ovens and smokers are not affected by the wood heater restrictions or the phase-out policy. Normal fire danger and nuisance smoke rules still apply.

My take

I love a real fire as much as anyone. We have a wood heater at home and use it most winter nights. For new projects, I'm honest with clients: the regulatory direction is clear even if the timing isn't, and the bioethanol and electric flame technology has improved to the point where the trade-off is much smaller than it was five years ago. Building a wood-heater-ready home now is also building in a removal cost later if the phase-out becomes binding. And on the rules themselves, I'd rather tell you what the legislation says than what the headlines say, which is why this article now carries its sources.

If you're renovating or building and want help thinking through the fireplace decision in the context of your wider project, the fastest path is a project consultation. Coffee's on me.
